Employee welcome kit checklist: what new joiners will actually use

Choose practical first-day essentials, plan personalisation and make repeat onboarding orders easier to manage.

01 / 04

Choose essentials for the first week

A good welcome kit supports the first days at work. A notebook, pen and water bottle are straightforward starting points; a tumbler or branded clothing can add value when employees are likely to use them. Keep the kit relevant to the work and avoid adding items simply to increase the piece count.

A short card from the team can make the welcome feel personal. Keep operational instructions and confidential company information in internal onboarding materials that can be updated, rather than printing details that may change on the gift packaging.

03 / 04

Gather branding details and sizes

Share the logo file, preferred placement and any brand-colour guidance. Ask which branding methods are suitable for each item; print quality and colour can vary by material. Review artwork proofs before approval and check that the logo remains legible at the proposed size.

If a kit includes clothing, decide how sizes will be collected and who will check them. For individual message cards, use a final spreadsheet with one spelling source and review a proof before production. Do not send personal employee data through an unagreed channel.

04 / 04

Plan packing, delivery and future batches

Confirm the hire date, recipient count, office locations and whether the kits should arrive before or on the first day. Ask about current stock and production time, especially if you expect new joiners throughout the year. A small buffer helps when start dates change.

Keep the approved contents, artwork and quote together as a repeat-order record. Before each later batch, reconfirm item availability, prices, sizes and branding. The product images illustrate the proposed presentation; ask for final contents and packing details to be confirmed in writing.
